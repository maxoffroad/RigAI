## Vehicle scope and generations

The Toyota 4Runner name spans distinct generations. Phase 4A focuses its technical guidance on the 5th Gen, model years 2010-2024. Toyota identifies the 2025 model as the start of the 6th Gen. The pages do not transfer 5th Gen fitment guidance to the 6th Gen, Land Cruiser Prado, or Lexus GX.

## First-upgrade concepts

The first-upgrades guide is a decision framework, not a product ranking. It starts with baseline condition and then considers tires, recovery readiness, targeted protection, suspension planning, and secondary equipment.

Important boundary: the guide does not state that every owner must buy the same first product. The order changes with maintenance condition, tire condition, terrain, experience, budget, and planned load.

## Safety and fitment considerations

- Planning guidance is not part-specific fitment confirmation.
- Model year, generation, trim, drivetrain, KDSS status, existing lift, accessories, front/rear load, tire and wheel plans, and installer requirements are explicit verification inputs.
- The pages direct users to manufacturer application data and installation instructions.
- No page promises that a lift creates tire clearance.
- No page says a specific supporting component is mandatory at a universal lift height.
- No service, recovery, or installation procedure is published.

## Confirmed facts

1. The 5th Gen Toyota 4Runner covers model years 2010-2024 according to Toyota.
2. The 2025 model begins the 6th Gen according to Toyota.
3. KDSS availability varies by configuration; Toyota documents it as an option on the 2024 TRD Off-Road.
4. Toyota describes KDSS as a hydraulic stabilizer-bar system with different road and off-road behavior.
5. Toyota owner documentation warns that non-genuine modifications can affect performance, safety, durability, regulations, warranty coverage, and active safety systems.
6. Toyota tells owners to use the owner's manual for weight limits and restrictions.
7. Damper architecture and load-matched spring selection are legitimate decision inputs, but they do not establish a universally best suspension.

## Claims excluded due to insufficient evidence

1. A universal maximum lift height that is safe for every 5th Gen configuration.
2. A universal tire size that fits every 5th Gen trim, wheel, alignment, and suspension setup.
3. A claim that a particular control arm, panhard correction, brake line, or bump stop is always required at a specific lift height.
4. Exact front or rear accessory-weight thresholds for selecting a spring.
5. A claim that monotube, twin-tube, or remote-reservoir dampers are always best.
6. A claim that every 5th Gen 4Runner has KDSS.
7. A claim that a lift guarantees tire clearance or improved off-road capability.
8. A fixed modification order for every owner.
9. Product rankings, brand rankings, and specific part fitment.
10. Exact budget tiers without market, region, and date research.
