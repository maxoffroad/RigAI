# Toyota 4Runner Phase 4B Research

## Research scope

Research was limited to four English-language 5th Gen Toyota 4Runner topics: KDSS, lift planning, tire-size selection, and overland-build planning. The work supports decision frameworks, not product rankings or universal fitment claims.

## Vehicle and generation scope

Toyota identifies the fifth generation as model years 2010-2024 and the sixth generation as beginning with model year 2025. Phase 4B guidance therefore focuses primarily on 2010-2024 vehicles. Model year, trim, market, drivetrain, KDSS status, wheels, load, and prior modifications remain vehicle-specific inputs.

## KDSS research

Toyota describes KDSS as hydraulically adjusting stabilizer-bar resistance. Toyota's 2024 material says the system can reduce stabilizer-bar resistance off road while retaining conventional stabilizer-bar behavior on road. The same source describes KDSS as optional on a named 2024 configuration, which supports the editorial rule that trim name alone is not proof for every year or market.

The page therefore explains system purpose at a high level, asks readers to confirm equipment from vehicle-specific records or qualified inspection, and requires manufacturer-specific KDSS fitment and installation information.

## Lift-kit research

Toyota's owner manual warns that non-genuine modifications can affect performance, safety, durability, regulations, and advanced safety systems. ARB's suspension-selection guidance supports choosing springs around actual vehicle load. Phase 4A's BILSTEIN source supports discussing damper architecture without declaring a universal winner.

The lift guide avoids an inches-to-tire-size table. It treats ride height as one output and separates it from geometry, usable travel, alignment, KDSS, actual load, and tire clearance.

## Tire-size research

NHTSA directs owners to the Tire and Loading Information label and owner documentation for manufacturer tire size and cold-pressure information. NHTSA recommends original-size tires or another size recommended by the vehicle manufacturer.

Michelin explains width, aspect ratio, construction, and rim diameter; it also notes that changing diameter can affect speedometer accuracy, clearance, and vehicle-system behavior. Michelin publishes approved rim-width ranges and model-specific dimensions, supporting the distinction between a nominal sidewall label and the exact tire specification.

No universal 4Runner fitment table was created. The page requires the exact placard, wheel data, tire specification, load and speed capacity, and dynamic clearance review.

## Overland-build research

Toyota's brochure instructs owners not to overload the vehicle, to secure roof loads, and to consult the owner's manual for weight limits and restrictions. NHTSA materials likewise identify the vehicle label as the source for capacity and tire information.

The guide does not publish one payload or roof-load number across the generation. It treats passengers, cargo, water, travel supplies, armor, bumper, winch, rack, and recovery equipment as parts of the operating-load calculation for the exact vehicle.

## Safety and fitment risks

- Treating trim name as proof of KDSS equipment.
- Applying a non-KDSS kit or procedure to a KDSS-equipped vehicle.
- Selecting lift by advertised height without checking load, travel, and geometry.
- Treating lift as proof of tire clearance.
- Treating nominal tire size as the full dimensional specification.
- Ignoring wheel width, offset, load rating, brake clearance, and rim-width range.
- Publishing one payload or roof-load number for every 5th Gen configuration.
- Giving step-by-step recovery or electrical instructions without the necessary training context.

## Conflicting information

- KDSS availability statements are often year-, trim-, and market-specific. No cluster-wide trim shortcut was published.
- Lift-height and tire-clearance claims vary across kit makers, tire models, wheels, alignment, vehicle load, and prior modifications. No universal combination was published.
- Payload and roof-load numbers vary by exact vehicle and equipment. No single generation-wide number was published.
- Tire category names such as “rugged terrain” are not standardized consistently across brands, so the page labels that category as manufacturer-defined.

## Claims excluded

1. A universal maximum lift height for KDSS.
2. A fixed lift height that requires a named supporting component on every vehicle.
3. An inches-to-tire-size fitment table.
4. Any promise about rubbing or clearance.
5. One factory tire size for every 5th Gen trim and year.
6. One payload or roof-load number for every 5th Gen 4Runner.
7. A claim that KDSS should be removed or disabled.
8. Product rankings, prices, or affiliate product blocks.
9. Step-by-step recovery procedures.
10. Detailed electrical sizing or wiring instructions.
